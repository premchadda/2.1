A shopkeeper marks his goods 40% above the cost price and then offers a 20% discount. What is his profit percentage on the sale?

💡 Need a hint? Get a nudge without revealing the answer.
💡 AI Hint

Think about what happens when you apply a 40% markup followed by a 20% discount — they don't cancel out. Try with ₹100 as the cost price.

✅
Correct!
You solved this in 42 seconds (avg 55s — you're faster than usual 🎉)
📖 Explanation

Let CP = ₹100. Marked price = ₹140. Discount = 20% of ₹140 = ₹28. Selling price = ₹140 − ₹28 = ₹112. Profit = ₹112 − ₹100 = ₹12, so profit % = 12%.

🧮 Formula Used AI
Profit % = ((SP − CP) / CP) × 100
SP = MP × (1 − Discount%/100)
⚠️ Common Mistake AI

Students often subtract 20% from 40% directly and get 20% profit.

This is wrong because the discount is applied on the marked price (₹140), not the cost price (₹100). Always compute step by step.

🎴 Concept Card AI
Shortcut
Net % = a − b − (ab/100). Here: 40 − 20 − (40×20/100) = 40 − 20 − 8 = +12%
Memory Trick
"Markup-then-discount never cancels" — always compute net with ab/100
Common Trap
Discount applies on MP, not CP
Formula
SP = CP × (1 + m/100)(1 − d/100)
